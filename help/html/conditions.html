Weather Conditions

Temperature and precipitation totals don't tell the whole story. WeatherSnake also fetches the daily WMO weather code from Open-Meteo and translates it into plain-language conditions, then summarizes which conditions dominate your chosen window historically.

The condition distribution

Every run (and the GUI's Conditions & Extremes panel) lists each condition category with the number of days and its share of the window, most common first:

Weather Conditions (historical)
------------------------------------------------------------
  Drizzle                  256 days    47.4%
  Overcast                 126 days    23.3%
  Rain                     123 days    22.8%
  ...
  Rain days (>=1 mm): 45.2% of days

"Drizzle 47.4%" means: across every occurrence of this window in the analysis depth, 47.4% of days were classified as drizzle. That is the answer to "what is this period usually like?" — not just how much rain fell.

Condition categories

Open-Meteo's weather codes are WMO interpretation codes. WeatherSnake groups them into these categories:

CategoryCodesMeaning
Clear sky0Cloudless
Mainly clear1Very light cloud
Partly cloudy2Broken cloud
Overcast3Full cover
Fog45, 48Fog and depositing rime fog
Drizzle51–55Light to dense drizzle
Freezing drizzle56, 57Drizzle at freezing temperatures
Rain61–65Slight to heavy rain
Freezing rain66, 67Rain at freezing temperatures
Snow71–77Snowfall and snow grains
Rain showers80–82Slight to violent showers
Snow showers85, 86Slight to heavy snow showers
Thunderstorm95–99Thunderstorm, with or without hail

Rain days

The line "Rain days (≥1 mm)" counts days with at least 1 mm of precipitation — a standard meteorological definition of a wet day. This is fixed and independent of the precipitation threshold.

Command line equivalent

python weather_juice.py --city Durban --period Summer --depth 10
# The conditions table prints by default; use --no-insights to skip it.
