Getting Started

Your first analysis takes less than a minute:

  1. Choose a location. Use the Location dropdown for the built-in presets (Cape Town, Johannesburg, Durban), or pick Custom… and type any place name the geocoder recognizes. See Choosing a Location.
  2. Choose a period. Summer, Autumn, Winter, Spring, Full Year, a single Month, or a Custom Range such as 15 Nov – 28 Feb. See Choosing a Period.
  3. Choose a depth. How many past years to average: 1, 3, 5, 7, 10, 15, or 20. See Analysis Depth.
  4. Press Fetch Data. WeatherSnake geocodes the location, downloads the daily history, filters it to your window, and draws the chart. The table on the left shows the averaged values; the summary below the chart shows conditions and extremes.
  5. Save or export. Save to JPG for the chart image, Export CSV for the numbers, or CSV + Infographic to publish an infographic (see Exporting Data).

Reading the chart

  • Red/orange lines (left axis) — average daily high and low for the window.
  • Blue bars (right axis) — average precipitation for the window.
  • The x-axis lists months (when Monthly Average is ticked) or individual day-of-year values.

Going deeper

  • Tick Conditions & Extremes for typical ranges, records, and the wettest/driest years (Conditions & Extremes).
  • Tick Yearly Breakdown for per-year values and trends (Yearly Breakdown).
  • Compare recent years against a longer baseline on the command line with --compare-recent (Recent vs Baseline).
Where does the data come from? The Open-Meteo Archive — a reanalysis product blended from weather-station observations. It needs no API key, but an internet connection is required to fetch.
