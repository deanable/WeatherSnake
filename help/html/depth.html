Analysis Depth

Depth is how many previous years of weather are folded into the averages. The allowed values are 1, 3, 5, 7, 10, 15, and 20.

With a depth of 10, a Summer fetch (run in 2026) averages the summers that started in 2016 through 2025 — ten complete occurrences of the window, never a partial one.

How deep should I go?

  • Depth 1–3 — "what were the last few years like?" Useful for recent conditions; sensitive to single unusual years.
  • Depth 5–10 — a good default. Smooths out one-off events while keeping the answer relevant to today's climate.
  • Depth 15–20 — the long view. Best for spotting shifts between eras, especially combined with Recent vs Baseline or a Yearly Breakdown.
Tip: averages hide variability. Keep Conditions & Extremes ticked so you also see typical ranges (5th–95th percentile), records, and the wettest/driest years — two depths with the same average can behave very differently.

Exact depth, complete windows

WeatherSnake analyzes exactly the depth you ask for. Cross-year windows (like Dec–Feb) fetch one extra leading year only so the earliest occurrence is complete; within-year windows don't. Either way, --depth 20 means twenty complete occurrences of your window.

Command line equivalent

python weather_juice.py --city "Cape Town" --period Winter --depth 20 --yearly
