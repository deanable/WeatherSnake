Data Sources & Accuracy

Where the data comes from

WeatherSnake uses the free Open-Meteo Archive API, which serves the ERA5 and ERA5-Land reanalysis datasets produced by the Copernicus Climate Change Service (ECMWF). Reanalysis blends millions of historical observations from weather stations, ships, aircraft, and satellites into a physically consistent gridded record reaching back to 1940. No API key is required.

What that means for accuracy

  • It's a grid, not a gauge. Values represent roughly an 9–30 km cell around your chosen coordinates. A specific backyard gauge can differ, but the grid is excellent for averages and patterns.
  • Recent days can be revised. The newest archive days (roughly the last five) may update as late-arriving observations are folded in. For stable results, note that yesterday's values may shift slightly on a later fetch.
  • Averages are the sweet spot. Single-day extremes in reanalysis are approximations of reality; multi-year averages — what WeatherSnake specializes in — are very reliable.

How WeatherSnake processes it

  1. Geocodes the location (Open-Meteo Geocoding API).
  2. Fetches daily temperature_2m_max, temperature_2m_min, precipitation_sum, and weather_code for the requested depth (cross-year windows fetch one extra leading year so the earliest occurrence is complete).
  3. Filters to the season or custom window, excludes February 29 everywhere.
  4. Averages per month or day-of-year, applies the precipitation threshold, computes condition distributions (Weather Conditions), variability and extremes (Conditions & Extremes), per-year trends (Yearly Breakdown), and comparisons (Recent vs Baseline).
  5. Converts to imperial units only at display time.
