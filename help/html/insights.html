Conditions & Extremes

The Conditions & Extremes checkbox adds the analytical summary under the chart: what the weather is usually like in the window, and how far from "usual" it can stray. Averages alone hide this; two windows with identical averages can behave very differently.

What each line means

Variability & Extremes
------------------------------------------------------------
  Typical daily high: 26.2°C (range usually 21.5°C to 31.1°C)
  Typical daily low:  18.9°C (range usually 15.2°C to 23.0°C)
  Record low/high in window: 9.4°C / 38.7°C

  Mean season total precipitation: 312.0 mm
  Wettest year:  2022 (589.0 mm)
  Driest year:   2018 (120.0 mm)
  Years above average: 4 of 10
  Rain days per year: 38 avg (max 61)
LineHow to read it
Typical daily high / lowThe mean, followed by the 5th–95th percentile range: 9 out of 10 days historically fall inside it. Plan around the range, not just the mean.
Record low/high in windowThe most extreme single days observed across all analyzed years. Useful for hard limits (frost risk, heat stress).
Mean season total precipitationThe average sum of rain over the whole window.
Wettest / driest yearThe occurrence of the window with the most/least total rain — the realistic best/worst case across your depth.
Years above averageHow many of the analyzed years exceeded the mean total; near half suggests steady behaviour, far from half suggests clustered wet/dry spells.
Rain days per yearDays with ≥ 1 mm per occurrence of the window, on average and at the maximum. A season can have high totals from few rainy days — or the opposite.
Combining views: tick Yearly Breakdown too for the year-by-year numbers behind these summary lines, and read them together with the condition distribution.

Command line equivalent

Variability and extremes print by default; skip them with --no-insights:

python weather_juice.py --city "Cape Town" --period Summer --depth 10
