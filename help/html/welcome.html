Welcome to WeatherSnake

WeatherSnake is a historical weather analyzer. It turns decades of daily weather records into practical insight — multi-year averages, the most common weather conditions, typical ranges and extremes, year-over-year trends, and recent-versus-baseline comparisons — so you can reason about future weather by reflecting on patterns of the past.

Weather data comes from the free Open-Meteo Archive API. No API key, registration, or subscription is required, and the archive reaches back to 1940.

What can it tell me?

  • Averages, done properly — temperature and precipitation averaged per month or per day-of-year across your chosen depth of years, with dew and frost excluded by a configurable precipitation threshold.
  • Weather-condition summaries — plain-language conditions (clear, overcast, drizzle, thunderstorm, …) derived from WMO weather codes, with a most-common- condition distribution and rain-day share for any window (see Weather Conditions).
  • Variability & extremes — typical daily high/low ranges (5th–95th percentile), record low/high, wettest and driest historical years, and rain days per year (see Conditions & Extremes).
  • Trends — a year-by-year breakdown with trend-per-decade estimates, and a recent-vs-baseline comparison to show whether things are shifting (see Yearly Breakdown and Recent vs Baseline).
  • Flexible windows — seasons, full year, a single month, or any custom day/month range that crosses year boundaries (see Choosing a Period).

Two ways to use it

  • The desktop window — pick a location, period, and depth, then press Fetch Data. Press F1 on any control for help about that control (see The WeatherSnake Window).
  • The command line — scriptable, with the same engine; see the CLI Reference.
Getting around this help: press F1 anywhere in the application for context-sensitive help, or browse the Contents tab. Every topic ends with links to related topics.
